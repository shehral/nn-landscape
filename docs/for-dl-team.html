Patterns this build

  • Open-weight models now lead document benchmarks: MiniMax M3 scores 91.6% on OmniDocBench 1.5, above Nanonets OCR-3's 90.5, and PP-OCRv6 claims competitive detection/recognition at under 35M parameters. The assumption that proprietary scale is required for benchmark-leading document understanding no longer holds.
  • Document VLM research is converging on efficiency rather than scale: arXiv 2609.01575 achieves large-model document quality with a 3B-active MoE architecture on a single H100, while PP-OCRv6 challenges billion-parameter VLMs with sub-35M parameter compact OCR. Two independent research lines in this build's window reached the same efficiency-first conclusion.
  • Mechanistic hallucination research is shifting from detection to causal intervention: three papers this build address VLM hallucinations via representation editing, circuit probing, and NTP-based probes rather than post-hoc filtering. The intervention-level approach maps more directly onto phantom-row mitigation than detection-only methods.
  • Table extraction is attracting new benchmarking infrastructure: TEAR (arXiv 2609.15205) introduces an attribute-aware evaluation protocol and the Open Tabular Insight survey (arXiv 2609.07629) argues current benchmarks miss structural complexity. Two independent papers in this window are establishing new evaluation standards for structured table understanding.
