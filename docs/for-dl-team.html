Visual Multi-Agent System: Mitigating Hallucination Snowballing via Visual Flow

ViF (ICLR 26) shows hallucinations propagate across VLM agent turns via vision-token attention decay; lightweight visual relay tokens mitigate this.

Product
Agentic document pipelines that relay VLM outputs across multiple processing steps are vulnerable to hallucination amplification at each turn. The ViF mitigation is inference-only and plug-and-play, applicable without retraining to any multi-step extraction workflow.
Research
The mechanism — vision tokens losing attention weight in deeper agent turns, leaving hallucinated text to propagate as authoritative — is distinct from single-instance hallucination. The attention-layer analysis could be replicated on OCR2-3B to locate equivalent degradation points in multi-turn extraction.

68 arxiv

Reading or Guessing? Visual Grounding Failures of Vision-Language Models for OCR in Ancient Greek Editions

Controlled image perturbations reveal VLMs produce fluent but visually unsupported OCR text; language-prior reliance measurable via conditional-vs-image-free decoding distributions.

Product
OCR outputs that appear fluent may be hallucinated from language priors rather than visual evidence, a production risk on low-quality scans or low-resource scripts. The failure mode is silent: the model produces plausible text rather than recognizable noise.
Research
The conditional-vs-image-free decoding distribution gap is a training-free grounding diagnostic. Applying it to OCR2-3B on degraded scans and non-Latin script documents would establish whether the model's OCR accuracy on such inputs is grounded or prior-driven.
