  • Data-centric document VLM training is converging on residual-guided curation: both WeVisDoc (Stage II error-guided loop) and arXiv:2609.01575 (difficulty-aware curation on prediction residuals) apply deployed-model confidence signals rather than annotation volume to identify high-value training examples.

Each targets a different document-economics profile.
  • Mechanistic interpretability tooling for VLMs is shifting from SAE-based probing to transcoder-based functional tracing: the Transcoders paper argues SAEs are structurally insufficient to isolate cross-modal updates, and When Language Overwrites Vision identifies a geometric suppression failure mode that SAE probing would not surface. Both findings change the tooling requirements for hallucination attribution research.

When Language Overwrites Vision: Over-Alignment and Geometric Debiasing in Vision-Language Models

Language-over-vision suppression in VLMs identified as a geometric bias; geometric debiasing technique reduces hallucinations caused by language-side over-alignment.

Product
Language-over-vision suppression is a plausible contributing mechanism for text hallucinations in OCR-VLMs where the language prior overrides the visual signal. The geometric debiasing technique requires no architectural change and is applicable as an inference-time or fine-tuning intervention.
Research
The suppression failure mode manifests specifically when the visual input contradicts a strong language prior — the OCR scenario (transcribing rare or low-frequency text strings) is a high-risk condition for this failure. The geometric debiasing approach is a testable intervention for phantom text generation.
