Patterns this build

  • General-purpose frontier vision models are approaching OCR price floors without matching OCR accuracy: Opus 5.5 costs ~$0.058/page and ranks #38/59 on OCR evals; Gemini 3.8 Flash at $0.75/MTok leads on finance/legal reasoning but trails on dense document extraction. The cost-accuracy trade-off that justifies dedicated OCR products remains, but the gap is narrowing on price.
  • SHROOM-Visions 2026 has established character-level hallucination detection as a distinct evaluation axis for VLMs, producing multiple competitive entries using internal VLM hidden states. F1-aggregate OCR benchmarks (OmniDocBench, IDP Leaderboard) measure different failure modes than the character-level fabrication SHROOM-Visions targets.
  • Structural priors are emerging as the primary technique for table hallucination suppression: PrismAlign's Bayesian rule-violation detection (row/column count constraints before cell content evaluation) reaches SOTA on OmniDocBench 1.5 table category. This complements rather than competes with the difficulty-aware curation approach in arXiv 2609.01575.
  • Multi-task reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards is becoming the standard training methodology for top-performing open document parsers: Infinity-Parser2 uses 8-objective joint RL to reach #1 on olmOCR-Bench and ParseBench. This is architecturally distinct from prior SFT-only approaches that top earlier leaderboard generations.
  • The competitive document-parsing market is bifurcating into per-page generalist models (Mistral OCR 4.1, OCR-3) and per-character specialist products (LandingAI DPT-3 Verity). Per-character pricing advantages sparse documents; per-page pricing advantages dense ones. Without a public OCR-3 pricing anchor, buyer comparisons default to competitor framing.
