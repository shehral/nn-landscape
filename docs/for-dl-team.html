When Prompts Override Vision: Prompt-Induced Hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models

Paper shows text prompts can override visual grounding in LVLMs, inducing hallucination of objects absent from the image—a failure mode distinct from object-frequency bias or phantom-row errors.

Product
In document extraction workflows, prompt templates encode expected output schemas. If prompts can override visual grounding independently of image content, schema-driven extraction may be systematically more vulnerable to hallucination than free-form transcription.
Research
Prompt-induced hallucination is a distinct failure mode from the team's current taxonomy of phantom-row, repetition-loop, structural, and cross-architecture hallucinations; the causal mechanism—prompt overriding visual grounding—warrants investigation against OCR-3's extraction endpoints.

DASH: Detection and Assessment of Systematic Hallucinations of VLMs

DASH distinguishes systematic (model-specific, reproducible) from incidental hallucinations in VLMs, introducing a scoring framework that quantifies which error types scale with deployment volume.

Product
Systematic hallucinations—reproduced consistently by a given model on specific input types—are more operationally damaging than stochastic errors because they accumulate predictably across document volumes. DASH's detection framework could be adapted for OCR-3 audit pipelines to identify high-risk document types.
Research
The systematic/incidental distinction refines the existing hallucination taxonomy; DASH's methodology for identifying per-model systematic error patterns is compatible with causal-scrubbing approaches and may surface failure modes not visible in aggregate benchmark scores.
