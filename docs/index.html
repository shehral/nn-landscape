Patterns this build

  • Sub-2B open-weight models now lead OmniDocBench v1.6: NaviDC-OCR (96.87, ~1.2B), OvisOCR2 (96.58, 0.8B), and MinerU2.5-Pro (95.69) all post scores above OCR-3's published 90.5 at a fraction of OCR-3's 35B MoE parameter count. End-to-end RL post-training on native-resolution crops is the common recipe separating current leaders from the field.
  • Frontier model pricing for vision input dropped below $1/M tokens this build: Gemini 3.8 Flash ($0.75/M) and three prior Flash releases narrow the cost moat separating specialized document-extraction APIs from general-purpose VLMs. GPT-6 Astra ($10/M) and Fable 5.1 ($10/M) are above the extraction-API cost tier but signal that multimodal input is a commodity input format rather than a specialized capability.
  • Competitors are stacking extraction and retrieval into end-to-end document intelligence suites: Reducto r-1 (single-pass parsing at 1¢/page) and Mistral Agentic Search (multi-step retrieval over extracted documents) both shipped in the last 30 days. The pattern is consolidation from extraction-only toward extraction-plus-retrieval, directly competing with the full-stack agents product tier.
  • VLM hallucination detection is converging on attention-head-level analysis without retraining: the Role-Break paper (AUROC 93.23 across 6 VLMs) and the Reliable Document VQA paper both read failure modes from internal representations rather than output comparisons. Both are architecture-agnostic and test on open-weight models in the sub-10B parameter range directly applicable to OCR2-3B.
  • OmniDocBench version fragmentation has created a benchmark comparison problem: NaviDC-OCR, OvisOCR2, and MinerU2.5-Pro all publish v1.6 scores (MGAM methodology, April 2026); OCR-3's 90.5 has no published version qualifier. OCR-3 launched April 2, eight days before the v1.6 update. Without a version annotation, customer-facing comparisons citing OCR-3's score against v1.6 results are potentially misleading.
