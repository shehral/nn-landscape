Patterns this build

  • Post-training and data curation are closing the quality gap between small open-weight models and large specialized systems: WeVisDoc-4B (95.38 OmniDocBench v1.6), Infinity-Parser2 (87.6% olmOCR-Bench), and the difficulty-aware curation paper all achieve competitive results using data-centric methods — residual-guided cluster targeting, multi-task RL reward design, and difficulty sampling — rather than architectural changes. All three models are under 8B parameters.
  • Inference-time and closed-loop techniques have emerged as a distinct approach to structural hallucination mitigation, independent of retraining: PrismAlign achieves table-category SOTA on OmniDocBench 1.5 via Bayesian multi-view alignment at inference, and OCR-EDR closes the loop on complex-document repair using rendering verification. Both treat the base VLM output as an editable state rather than a terminal one, enabling deployment without model changes.
  • Frontier model pricing compression continues: GPT-6 Luna ($0.10/$0.50/MTok) and Reducto r-1 ($0.01/page flat) represent the new floor for image-capable frontier APIs and specialized document parsers respectively. Three consecutive builds have recorded a downward pricing movement in the cheapest viable option; the per-document cost gap between specialized and commodity APIs is narrowing on sparse-content document types.
  • Document AI competitive moats are shifting from accuracy to distribution channels and cost structure: Docling MCP is listed in the Claude connector directory with one-click slash-command workflows, Samsung's chip-fabrication partnership with Mistral targets long-term inference cost structure, and Claude Opus 5.5 scores weakly on OCR (#38 of 59) on a developer-facing comparison surface where OCR-3 is not listed. Distribution presence and cost-structure trajectory are becoming as significant as benchmark accuracy in developer vendor selection.
  • Scientific and multilingual document understanding benchmarks are diversifying evaluation surfaces beyond OmniDocBench: SciDocBench (5 scientific domains, 7 capability groups), MDPBench (17 languages, photographed documents, NeurIPS 2026), and the SHROOM-Visions character-level hallucination span benchmark each cover dimensions OmniDocBench aggregates away. The strongest tested model scores below 63/100 on SciDocBench, suggesting measurable headroom for specialized extractors on non-standard document types.
